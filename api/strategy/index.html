The Partners Page

A landing page for partner referrals.

Brokers, financial planners, accountants and conveyancers refer their clients to API. This is the page those referrals land on, and the page partners point to as public proof of the relationship.

Role 01
The warm-referral landing pad.
When a broker tells their client "I trust API for property consultancy work," the client lands here, sees the broker's name listed, and the verbal referral becomes verified. Trust is pre-loaded. Conversion is higher than cold traffic ever gets.
Role 02
Public proof of the relationship.
Partners get a public page they can point clients to. "Here's who I trust." The relationship becomes visible instead of informal, which makes it more durable. It also signals to future potential partners that the network is a real, named thing.
Role 03
Two-way linking.
Every partner site that links to API, and that API links back to, compounds entity authority on AI search. The Partners page is where this happens at scale. The more partners in the network, the stronger the signal.

Findability Strategy
How AI search finds us

When prospects ask AI for a buyer's agent, you come up.

When someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "who's a good buyer's agent in Sydney for rentvestors," you already show up. You saw it happen in January, when one of your clients recommended you to her sister and ChatGPT verified the recommendation. Two things make that work, and both run quietly in the background.

01 · Make Emma legible to AI
AI systems need confirmation before they cite someone. The Founder page becomes Emma's official online source. Every link from her LinkedIn, Crunchbase, podcast, anthology, and the 10 named partners points back to it. Behind the scenes, structured data tells AI exactly who she is and how she connects to the company, the books, and reviews.
02 · Quiet background work
The site speaks AI's language behind the scenes. A small file at the root of the site (called llms.txt) tells AI crawlers what to read and cite. Structured metadata makes every page machine-readable. None of it shows in the visible design. None of it needs Emma's attention. It just runs.

Findability Strategy
How keywords work for us

Three tiers.

Some searches exist already, some don't. The strategy treats them differently, and stacks them in the right order.

01 · Capture demand that exists
Real-volume, winnable terms (per Thursday's live Semrush). "How to buy first home" (~8,100 monthly searches, keyword difficulty 4) — high volume, very low difficulty, a major content opportunity. "Rentvesting" (~2,400 / month, keyword difficulty 21) — winnable, feeds the eBook funnel. "Property consultant" (~390 / month) and "property strategist" (~260 / month) — lower volume but more aligned to how API positions. "Buyer's advocate" — Emma is technically licensed as one; worth a content mention without leading with it. "Buyer's agent [city]" deprioritised — keyword difficulty too high and the terminology doesn't match how API operates.
02 · Convert with differentiators
Female-led + the readiness quiz + new-property specialism. None of these have meaningful direct search volume, but they're what closes the visitor SEO brings in. The female-led story and new-property specialism (post-budget, materially stronger) run through the main pages as differentiators. The readiness quiz becomes the first step for prospects — replacing "book a discovery call" with something more engaging.
03 · Build branded demand over time
API as new-property specialists, female-led, with a process. Used consistently across content, webinars, and Emma's events, these grow into branded search demand API creates. People who hear Emma speak or read an eBook later search for "Active Property Investing" or "Emma Allen" directly. Strengthens AI-assistant visibility — distinctive named positions get cited over generic content.
"How to buy first home" + rentvesting content first to build authority and show early results  ·  then property-consultant / property-strategist commercial pages once authority compounds.

Findability Strategy
The migration safety net

Nothing that's already converting gets broken.

01 · URL preservation
1:1 redirect map at launch. Every URL on the current site → matched to its new URL, or kept identical. Manual launch-day verification of every redirect. No silent 404s. No ranking left on a dead page.
02 · Entity preservation
Every external link updated at launch. LinkedIn, Crunchbase, RocketReach, every podcast guest-appearance backlink, the 10 named partner firm backlinks, Property Boss Mums cross-references. The web of links that already verifies Emma stays intact.
03 · Analytics continuity
Google Analytics + Google Search Console installed on the current site Week 1. Same property persists through migration. We land with a baseline, not zero data. Microsoft Clarity captures pre-launch behavioural patterns to compare against.
Urgent action
Google Analytics is not currently installed on the live site. Every week of pre-launch Google Analytics + Google Search Console data is migration safety net we don't have yet. The install is a 30-minute Week 1 task.

Photography direction

People in places. Not properties as products.

What we shoot in priority order
  • People in places. Emma + clients + team in real spaces. Reportage-leaning, eye-level, natural light, calm expressions. Treated like editorial profile photography (think New Yorker profile, not corporate headshot).
  • Architecture as place. When we show properties, we show them as places where lives happen, not as listings. Streetscapes, neighbourhood context, light through windows.
  • Objects of the work. The readiness scale on Emma's wall, open notebooks with handwritten suburb notes, anthology covers. Documentary-style.
What we don't shoot category clichés
  • Aerial drone shots of suburbs (category default)
  • Stock "professional couple smiling at laptop"
  • Hands shaking on documents
  • House-sold signs
  • Posed team headshots against neutral backgrounds
  • Generic Sydney Harbour Bridge / Opera House
  • Image-library photography on hero surfaces
The most important asset
Emma's photography is the single most important visual asset in the build. The Founder-page hero is the lever that makes "Emma is the brand" actually land. One half-day editorial shoot in Phase 1 is the recommended commitment.
